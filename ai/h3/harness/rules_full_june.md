## Full Rules

This is the exact reference sheet for the current game engine. The quick rules teach the game; this page settles edge cases.

## Setup

There are three players: red, blue, and green.

The center space is a neutral jewel display and is not playable.

The pool has 5 slots. Each player's hand has 3 slots.

Each player starts with one seeded starter jewel in their hand. When that jewel is placed on the board, it creates that player's initial aura.

## Deck

The game uses a shuffled 100-tile deck built from red, green, blue, gray, and black sections.

Solids: 5 tile types, 4 copies each, for 20 tiles. A solid tile has all 6 edges the same color.

Bicolors: 10 tile types, 3 copies each, for 30 tiles. A bicolor tile has one 3-edge section of each of two colors.

Imbalanced tricolors: 20 tile types, 2 copies each, for 40 tiles. An imbalanced tricolor has one color on 4 edges and another color on 2 edges.

Balanced tricolors: 10 tile types, 1 copy each, for 10 tiles. A balanced tricolor has three colors, each covering 2 edges.

Altogether, the deck has 45 unique tile types. Starter jewels are seeded into player hands and are not drawn from the deck.

## Goal

Finish the game with the most points. The highest score wins. Ties are allowed.

## Turns

On a turn, a player picks up one tile from the deck, their hand, or the pool. They may rotate the tile before placing it.

A tile may be placed onto the board, into the player's hand, or into the pool, as long as that destination is legal.

A selected deck tile must be resolved before the player selects another tile.

Only a hand tile placed onto the board starts hand-chain play. Moving a hand tile into the pool does not start the hand chain.

Occupied board spaces cannot be overwritten unless Endgame Overwrite is enabled and the board is full.

## Deck And Pool

Before making a placement, a player may flip a deck tile and place it into any open pool slot.

After placing a deck tile into the pool, they may flip another deck tile. This can continue until the 5 pool slots are full.

Once the pool is full, the next flipped deck tile must be placed onto the board, placed into hand, or used with PASS to clear the pool.

Once a deck or pool tile is placed onto the board or into hand, the turn ends.

If a deck tile cannot legally be kept in hand or pool, it must be placed on the board if a legal board placement exists.

## Hand Play

If a player begins their turn by placing a hand tile onto the board, they may continue placing hand tiles onto the board until their hand is empty.

Because a hand has 3 slots, a full hand-chain can place up to 3 hand tiles.

A player may use PASS to stop hand play early. Remaining hand tiles stay in hand.

When a player empties their hand through board placements, they earn one bonus pool tile if a legal pool tile is available.

The bonus pool tile acts like a hand tile for board-placement rules, but it must be placed directly onto the board. It cannot be stored in hand.

## Passing

If the pool is full and the player has selected a flipped deck tile, PASS discards the full pool plus that deck tile and ends the turn.

PASS can also end hand play early or decline an earned bonus pool tile. Those hand-play opt-outs do not count toward the three-pass game end.

Three consecutive no-placement or pool-clearing PASS turns end the game.

## Placement

A tile may be placed on any open board space, except the center space, if every edge touching another tile is legal.

Matching colors may touch edge-to-edge.

Gray is wild against normal colors and may touch a newly placed black edge, but gray does not bypass an already-placed black edge.

Black is wild when placed. After black is on the board, only black may touch that black edge.

## Scoring

Players score when tile sections of their color are linked to matching sections across tile edges.

Think of each 1/6th tile section as 1 point. A linked tile can contribute 2, 3, 4, or 6 points depending on how much of that tile's color is linked.

One tricolor section is worth 2. One bicolor section is worth 3. Two same-color tricolor sections are worth 4. A solid or jewel tile is worth 6.

Gray and black do not score and do not create scoring links.

Isolated color sections do not score until they link to another tile by a matching edge.

Scores are recalculated from the whole board after each placement.

## Initial Aura

Each player's initial jewel captures the 6 spaces around it when placed on the board. These spaces are that player's initial aura.

A starter jewel may not be placed where its initial aura would overlap another initial jewel aura or the protected center ring.

A player may place freely inside their own aura. Normal edge-matching rules do not block that placement.

Only one seeded starter jewel per player may be placed and scored.

## Opponent Aura

A player may not normally place a tile in another player's aura.

Exception: a player may place a hand tile into another player's aura if the tile follows normal board-placement rules.

Deck and pool tiles cannot enter another player's aura directly.

## Expanded Aura

When a tile is placed in a player's own aura, that tile can expand the player's aura.

Any open space touching that player's color on the aura-placed tile becomes part of the expanded aura.

Only the player's own color edges project aura. Gray, black, and opponent-colored edges do not project aura for that player.

Expanded auras may overlap, including over initial aura spaces.

When auras compete, the color with the most aura claims touching the space controls it. Ties make the space neutral again.

A starter jewel creates the first aura claims by itself; after that, those claims compete like any other aura claim.

## End Of Game

Classic rules end when the board is filled, or when no available tile can legally be played.

Endgame Overwrite rules: when the board is filled, hand tiles may still overwrite occupied board spaces. If the board fills during a hand-chain, that player is still finishing the same regulation hand turn: they may play remaining hand tiles as overwrites, PASS, or take the normal empty-hand pool bonus if earned; they may not flip the overtime deck during that carried turn.

If the pool is already full on a later overtime turn, that pool is the choice set. Otherwise, if the deck is flipped, deck tiles reveal into as many open pool slots as remain. The player must choose one pool tile and place it on the board or in an open hand slot, or PASS to discard the pool. Choosing an overtime pool tile ends the turn; it does not start a hand-chain. Resolving that first pool choice clears the remaining pool, so later overtime deck flips get all 5 pool slots filled.

Board placements overwrite occupied spaces, jewels cannot be overwritten, and overwritten tiles are discarded. The game ends when no tiles remain or three no-placement or pool-clearing PASS turns happen in a row.
